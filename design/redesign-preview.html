The Brief

From clip-art devotion to an illuminated manuscript of the everyday.

Devotional software in Hindi-speaking markets has, for too long, been a contest between garish kitsch and the sterile, flattened grid of generic Silicon-Valley UI. The former shouts; the latter apologises. Neither earns reverence.

This redesign argues for a third way — one that treats the app like a pothi, a hand-bound scripture: warm paper, measured rule, restrained ornament. Devanāgarī is typeset, not decorated. Astronomical times are set in steady monospace, as a ship's almanac would print them. Mandalas appear only where they earn their presence: as structure, never as stickers.

I.

Parchment, not plastic

A single warm ground across all surfaces. No white boxes floating on beige — the paper is the paper.

II.

Typeset Devanāgarī

Hindi leads every hierarchy. Serif-with-serif pairing. The Latin script plays support, never shout.

III.

Ornament with purpose

Yantras as structure. Lotus as rule-break. Gold leaf reserved for the card that matters most.

IV.

Calm, data-dense

A tithi is data. Sunrise is data. Treat them with the quiet dignity of an almanac, not a slot machine.

Screen Study · I

The home as a daily almanac.

Every morning, the user opens to a spread that reads like the first page of a hand-bound almanac. The date is set in italic serif with tabular numerals. Panchang — the five limbs of the day — occupies the hero card in ink-dark treatment: a brief escape from the parchment surface, as if the planets demand their own sky.

  • 01Italic serif greeting pairs with a Devanāgarī echo below — bilingual, not redundant.
  • 02Hero Panchang inverts to dark: gold-leaf rules, monospaced almanac times, a dim mandala watermark.
  • 03Deity chips are portrait medallions — initialed Devanāgarī, warm gradient grounds, bone-white ring.
  • 04The featured aarti card teases its opening verse — a reason to tap beyond a thumbnail.
  • 05Tab bar floats as a dark pill; active tab earns a gold ember.

Panchāng as ephemeris.

Month grid overlays a lightweight Devanāgarī tithi sub-label on every date — a quiet astronomical signature the user learns to read over time. Selected day emerges in deep sindoor. Quality windows (rāhu kāla, abhijit) earn colour only on their values, never their rows.

Screen Study · III

The pantheon, typeset.

A deity is not a sticker — it is a portrait, a halo, a mood. Each card takes a colour grammar from its deity (ochre-sindoor for Gaṇeśa, blue-twilight for Kṛṣṇa), the initial Devanāgarī set large, the Latin rendered in italic swash. The content count is the only data visible before you tap through.

Jaya Gaṇeśa Devā
Verse I · refrain
जय गणेश जय गणेश, जय गणेश देवा ।
माता जाकी पार्वती, पिता महादेवा ॥
Victory to Gaṇeśa, Lord of the gaṇas —
whose mother is Pārvatī, whose father, the great god.
Verse II
एकदन्त दयावन्त, चार भुजाधारी ।
माथे सिन्दूर सोहे, मूसे की सवारी ॥
Single-tusked, compassionate, four-armed —
sindūr adorns the brow; his mount, a humble mouse.
Verse III
पान चढ़े फूल चढ़े, और चढ़े मेवा ।
लड्डुअन का भोग लगे, सन्त करें सेवा ॥
Betel, flowers, and fruits are offered —
laddus in oblation; the devoted in service.

Screen Study · IV

The liturgy, in two tongues.

Devanāgarī verse leads, set large enough to chant from. The refrain is marked in sindoor red — a convention borrowed from old printed aartīs. English translation sits quietly in italic serif, for comprehension, not performance. The player floats as a dark vessel with a gold-leaf progress thread.
